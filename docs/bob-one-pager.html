Bob

One status card per project. One command that reads them all and tells you what to work on today.

Needs Claude Code on a Mac
R on your PATH (Rscript)
Five minutes to install

What it is

A scheduler that lives inside Claude Code. Every project you register carries a short Markdown card, bob.md. Bob reads all of them in one pass, weighs each deadline against the hours you actually have and how much the project matters, and gives you a plan: this morning, this week, this quarter.

What it never does. Research, code, drafting. Bob plans; you work; the card gets updated so tomorrow's plan is right. If it isn't on the card, Bob doesn't know it happened.

The loop

The card: six fields that place a project

blocking_otherssomeone waits on youbumps priority, BLOCKING badge

Below the fields: This Week, Upcoming Milestones, and Start Here Next Session, the note your next session opens with. Present tense, under 150 lines. History goes in docs/logs/; working rules go in CLAUDE.md.

A working day: four commands

/bobMorning, in a hub session from ~/.claude: hours available, what's red, three or four focus tasks, the important thing nobody is chasing. /bob go <project>Copies a launch line. New terminal tab, paste, Enter: a fresh session inside the project, with its own instructions and a five-line pickup from the card. /bob update <project>Before closing the tab: what changed goes on the card, the story goes into a dated log. /bob weeklyFriday: hours next week, what's red, what has quietly slipped, what's coming in three months.

Also: /bob add (register, short interview), grants, scan, remind, event, ideas, /bob <project>.

Why bother

  • One view across everything. Grants, papers, courses, admin, the exploratory thing: one table, sorted by what hurts first.
  • Urgency doesn't win by default. Importance is its own field; the important project with no deadline is surfaced on purpose, louder when it goes stale.
  • Clean switching. Each project's session starts with the right instructions and nothing carried over.
  • A handoff that survives you. The card tells a collaborator, or their agent, where things stand without a meeting.

Shared projects

  • One card per project, never one per person. A steward owns the top; everyone else edits only their own ## Person: section.
  • Your daily shows your part: your tasks, the project state, who waits on whom, and a line when a teammate changed theirs.
  • A mailbox, .bob/mail.md, for notes between people and agents. Append only; read state stays on your machine.
  • Rename your Bob if you like. Cards are found by a header tag, not by filename.

Get it

git clone https://github.com/petertanksley/bob-scheduler.git
cd bob-scheduler
./install.sh

Then two lines in ~/.claude/settings.json under permissions (copy from settings.permissions.example.json): additionalDirectories for the folders your projects live in, and the allow rule for Bob's collector script. Without them every card read asks permission.

New session, /bob add in a project folder, answer roughly: "about 10 hours, 2 a week" is plenty. Three cards in, run /bob.
